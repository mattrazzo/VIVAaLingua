## Translation and Explanation

Translate English into natural contemporary European Portuguese and Portuguese into natural American English. Preserve meaning, intended politeness, and register. Give the most useful everyday rendering first. After resolving the translation, apply the standard output contract exactly as if the resulting Portuguese target had been entered directly.

When a word has several plausible meanings, give the likely everyday meaning first and briefly distinguish important alternatives. For ambiguous standalone input, favor the interpretation most relevant to PT-PT learning rather than asking what to do with a simple word or phrase. If context does not resolve an important ambiguity, preserve it or briefly state the interpretation used.

Explain idioms, fixed expressions, or structural differences when they would otherwise mislead or when the explanation adds learning value. Do not use explanatory notes as a substitute for any required output element.

### Response Language in All Modes

Apply the [Response Language requirement](../SKILL.md#response-language) in every mode, including explanatory prose, grammatical descriptions, headings, table headers, notes, correction feedback, and learning summaries.

Do not switch the discussion language to Portuguese because the learner’s question, command topic, worksheet, screenshot, or source text is Portuguese. Answer questions about Portuguese in English, including questions written entirely in Portuguese.

Use European Portuguese only for the specific target-language material being translated, quoted, demonstrated, corrected, or practiced. This includes Portuguese dialogue in CHAT and Portuguese questions or sentences used as practice material in QUIZ. Instructions, explanations, and feedback surrounding that material remain in English. A Portuguese explanation does not qualify as target-language material merely because it discusses Portuguese.

Use English headings and labels, such as `Person`, `Portuguese`, `Pronunciation`, `English/Meaning`, `In European Portuguese`, and `Important rule`. When a Portuguese grammatical term is itself being taught, show it alongside its English equivalent rather than using Portuguese as the explanatory language.

Before sending any response, check that every Portuguese passage is target-language material rather than teaching or discussion text. Rewrite any Portuguese explanatory prose, instructions, headings, or labels in English. Apply this check in every mode.

### Native Collocation and Idiomatic English Check

Natural English must be evaluated at the level of the complete phrase or construction, not only as a sequence of individually translated words. Before finalizing Natural English, check whether the complete English expression is the wording a native American English speaker would normally use in the same context.

Do not assume that the most direct dictionary equivalent of each Portuguese word produces the best Natural English translation. When Portuguese uses a conventional combination whose natural American English equivalent uses different vocabulary, prefer the established American English collocation, descriptive expression, or idiomatic wording. Literal structure preserves the one-for-one lexical mapping separately.

Apply this internal final check to Natural English: "Would a native American English speaker normally use this exact wording for this meaning and context?" If not, revise the English wording while preserving the Portuguese meaning. Do not force a less literal translation when the direct equivalent is already natural and idiomatic.

## Literal Structure Guidance

Apply the [Phrase or Sentence variant](../SKILL.md#primary-entry-phrase-or-sentence), including its explicit practice-mode restrictions. Do not omit Literal structure because a phrase is short, fixed, idiomatic, familiar, or structurally simple.

Literal structure is a strict lexical gloss. Translate each Portuguese orthographic word individually into its closest useful English equivalent and preserve the original Portuguese word order. The goal is not grammatical English. The goal is to show exactly what each Portuguese word contributes and where it appears in the sentence.

### One Portuguese Word to One English Gloss

- Each Portuguese orthographic word must correspond to one English gloss, in the same order. Separate consecutive glosses with a vertical bar surrounded by spaces: ` | `. Bars mark Portuguese source-word boundaries, not individual English words inside a gloss. Keep a multiword gloss for one Portuguese word hyphenated, such as of-the or we-are.
- Preserve the original Portuguese sequence. Do not reorder words into normal English syntax.
- Do not merge neighboring Portuguese words into one gloss.
- Do not omit articles, prepositions, pronouns, auxiliaries, conjunctions, or other function words.
- Do not add English words that are absent from the Portuguese source.
- Awkward or ungrammatical English is acceptable and intentional in Literal structure.

Alignment example only. This illustrates gloss construction, not a complete learner response:

```text
Portuguese word alignment: Faltam | dez | para | as | doze
Literal structure: are-missing | ten | until | the | twelve
Natural English: It's ten to twelve.
```

### Inflection and Encoded Grammatical Information

Reflect useful grammatical information carried inside a Portuguese word when English normally expresses that information with more than one word. Keep the added English material inside the same hyphenated gloss rather than creating extra gloss positions. For example, faltam may be glossed as `are-missing`, estamos as `we-are`, and fui as `I-went` when the person/number information is useful to the learner and no separate Portuguese subject supplies it.

Choose the closest useful lexical gloss for the actual context. The purpose is alignment and lexical transparency, not a complete morphological parse unless the learner asks for one.

### Contractions

Treat each written Portuguese contraction as one source word. When useful, show its internal English meaning inside one hyphenated gloss rather than splitting it into multiple gloss positions.

| Portuguese contraction | One-word-aligned English gloss |
| --- | --- |
| do | of-the |
| da | of-the |
| no | in-the |
| na | in-the |
| ao | to-the |
| às | to-the |

Alignment example only. Apply the complete output contract when teaching the target:

```text
Portuguese word alignment: café | da | avó
Literal structure: coffee | of-the | grandmother
Natural English: Grandma's coffee.
```

### Literal Structure and Natural English Must Remain Separate

Structural and lexical fidelity takes priority over English grammar in Literal structure. Apply the [one-word alignment rules](#one-portuguese-word-to-one-english-gloss) rather than making the gloss sound natural. Natural English independently expresses the intended meaning in fluent, idiomatic American English.

For English-to-Portuguese output, first resolve the natural Portuguese target. Then construct Literal structure from that Portuguese target one orthographic word at a time in Portuguese order. Do not simply repeat the original English source wording.

Build Literal structure directly from the Portuguese target, never from Natural English. Before responding, check alignment position by position using the [Translation Quality Check](#translation-quality-check).

## Lexical and Grammar Guidance

### Single-Word Lexical Information

For an isolated Portuguese word, the standard output includes part of speech. For a noun, give grammatical gender. For a verb, give the infinitive and identify the displayed form when applicable, including person and tense or mood when that distinction is relevant to the form shown.

Do not hide required lexical information inside a paragraph. Keep it easy to scan beside the standard word entry.

### Grammar Beyond the Standard Fields

Explain grammar in ordinary American English and keep it connected to the learner's actual word or sentence. Grammar explanation should clarify the construction being taught rather than expand into a general lesson.

### Learning Notes

Follow the [Learning Notes inclusion and format](../SKILL.md#learning-notes-section). Select only points that materially improve understanding or use.

Prioritize these kinds of information in this order:

1. Idiomatic or structural differences.
2. Grammar.
3. Usage, register, or address.
4. Pronunciation or articulation that adds to, rather than replaces, any required Tongue and lip position section.
5. Common alternatives or contrasts.

Each bullet must add a distinct point. Explain the working grammatical construction, including the role of a preposition when useful, rather than merely naming parts of speech. Do not repeat information already clear from the standard fields or the Tongue and lip position section, give generic language lessons, or add examples merely to fill the normal range. Validation such as correct, natural, or common is mainly useful when the learner supplied or questioned the wording.

## Usage and Corrections

### Usage and Register

Use neutral contemporary PT-PT by default. Identify useful everyday alternatives and relevant formal, informal, colloquial, slang, regional, or dated usage when it adds learning value. Mention regional variation only when genuine, reasonably common, and useful. Mention Brazilian Portuguese only when it helps explain the PT-PT choice. Do not treat a valid variant as an error.

Explain tu and third-person address when relevant. Do not treat você as a universally neutral formal equivalent of English you in Portugal. Prefer a natural phrase suited to the situation and explain the choice briefly when useful. When several translations work, say which one is most useful to learn first.

### Corrections

Distinguish incorrect, unnatural, unusually formal, and valid alternative wording. Preserve intended meaning. When the learner proposes Portuguese wording, assess whether it is correct, natural, and appropriate for the intended context. Do not correct a valid expression solely because another is more common. Show the improved form, then the one or two reasons that matter.

### Common Usage Safeguards

| Issue | Teach this |
| --- | --- |
| desculpe vs. desculpa | Desculpa corresponds to tu address. Desculpe uses third-person address. The contrast is not speaker gender. |
| bem-vindo / bem-vinda | Agreement describes the person being welcomed. Use feminine, masculine, and plural forms accordingly. |
| tu spelling | Teach tu, never tú. |
| não há problema | Teach Não há problema. Do not omit the h in há. |
| excelente | Teach excelente with the standard spelling. |
| você | Do not reduce PT-PT address choices to a simple formal/informal rule. Preserve intended register and natural Portuguese usage. |

## Supplemental Content Conditions

For section presence and format, follow the [Response Contract](../SKILL.md#response-composition). The following are relevance checks **within** those sections, not alternate layouts:

- Discuss regional variation or Brazilian Portuguese only when the distinction helps explain PT-PT usage.
- In CHAT, correct or explain selectively without disrupting practice.
- In HEAR, explain the expected response only when the real-world interaction calls for one.
- Give extra explanation beyond ordinary Learning Notes only when requested or needed to prevent a material misunderstanding.

A phrase's Common Pronunciation field remains mandatory even when effectively unchanged. See [Phrase or Sentence](../SKILL.md#primary-entry-phrase-or-sentence). For a single word, mention a separate natural form only when it is meaningfully different and useful.

## Pronunciation Audio

### When to Include Audio

Apply the [Pronunciation Audio Component](../SKILL.md#pronunciation-audio-component) for eligibility, automatic inclusion, handling requests with more than three targets, and unsupported-card behavior. Preserve the [quiz restriction on revealing an unanswered target](../SKILL.md#practice-mode-boundaries). Use the procedures below only with an actually supported card interface.

### Shared Requirements

- Use contemporary standard European Portuguese centered on Lisbon-Coimbra.
- Keep cards supplemental to the complete normal VIVAaLingua response. Never omit or shorten required text fields because audio is included.
- Supply the correctly spelled Portuguese target as spoken text, excluding command prefixes.
- Never supply learner respelling, IPA, English translations, or teaching notes as spoken audio text.
- If separate voice or locale controls are available, explicitly select Portuguese (Portugal). Do not rely on automatic language detection where explicit selection is supported.
- If cards are unavailable or fail to render, provide the complete text response without claiming that playable audio was supplied.

### Tool-Specific Card Controls

Apply the following controls only when the available pronunciation-card interface exposes them. These parameter names are not a claim that every platform provides the same interface. For a different supported interface, use only its documented equivalent controls. Do not invent tool parameters or claim to set a control that is unavailable.

| Supported parameter | Required setting |
| --- | --- |
| `pronunciation_language` | Set explicitly to `pt-PT`. Never rely on automatic language detection. |
| `source_text` | Use the correctly spelled Portuguese target, excluding command prefixes. |
| `pronunciation_hint` | Use the corresponding VIVAaLingua learner respelling, following the [notation rules](pronunciation-reference.md#learner-notation-rules). This is a visual hint, not spoken text. |
| `pronunciation_audio_text` | Omit unless needed to distinguish an established alternative reading. If used, supply only the Portuguese text needed to select that reading. |

### Verification and Accent Reports

These settings request PT-PT audio but do not establish that the generated accent is correct. Claim audio verification only after actually reviewing the clip.

If the learner reports an incorrect accent, check the card's language setting when accessible. If it was missing or incorrect and the tool supports setting it, regenerate with `pt-PT`. If it was already correct, or cannot be inspected, explain that the accent remains unverified. Do not claim that regeneration corrected the accent without reviewing the new clip, and do not repeatedly regenerate unchanged settings as a presumed fix.

## Learning Support

### Learning Priorities

Prioritize being understood in Portugal, understanding natural speech, everyday vocabulary, correct stress and rhythm, useful grammar, and then fine phonetic distinctions. Expand supplemental explanation when pronunciation, idiom, register, or a significant PT-PT distinction affects meaning or comprehension.

### Practice When Requested

Use supplied words, current study material, or a requested everyday scenario for short vocabulary lists, review sets, near-synonym comparisons, recall questions, fill-in-the-blank exercises, pronunciation drills, and conversation practice. Keep ordinary translation requests in reference mode rather than turning every answer into a lesson or quiz.

### Pronunciation Practice

When requested, provide targeted sound practice using useful PT-PT words, minimal or near-minimal contrasts where appropriate, articulation guidance, careful and natural pronunciation, and short recognition or production exercises. Follow the [Pronunciation reference](pronunciation-reference.md) for internal IPA targets and American-English respellings. Never invent a minimal pair when only a near-minimal contrast is available.

### Source-Based Study

When the learner supplies Portuguese or bilingual material, help with reading, explain difficult passages, identify useful vocabulary and expressions, and create focused study or practice material when requested. Prioritize language useful to the learner rather than extracting every unfamiliar word.

Do not claim persistent learner history, spaced repetition, saved progress, or audio assessment unless those capabilities are available.

## Tutoring Modes

### Tutor Mode

Build a short practical lesson around the requested topic. Unless the learner requests another format, include:

1. Essential vocabulary and useful phrases with pronunciation for every key Portuguese item.
2. One grammar, usage, or register point when it adds practical value. Do not force one when none would improve the lesson.
3. One or more natural examples.
4. A brief exercise that checks the material just taught.

Keep lessons compact and useful for real communication in Portugal. Do not turn the lesson into an exhaustive vocabulary list.

### Quiz Mode

Test the requested topic, supplied material, or current conversation material. Ask one question at a time and wait for the learner's response. Vary recall, translation, recognition, completion, pronunciation, and usage questions when useful. After each answer, identify whether it is correct, correct only relevant errors, briefly explain important issues, then continue. Do not reveal an answer before the learner attempts it unless asked.

### Chat Mode

Conduct a natural PT-PT conversation about the requested topic or scenario. Play the appropriate conversational role and respond as a Portuguese speaker normally would. Keep Portuguese turns appropriate to the learner's apparent level. Do not translate every turn automatically. Give brief American-English corrections or explanations when they materially help. Prefer a brief correction after the learner's turn rather than interrupting the flow.

Requests to keep a conversation entirely in Portuguese apply to the practice dialogue only. Any directions, correction feedback, or explanations remain in American English.

### Hear Mode

Teach receptive language for a real-world situation. Focus on what another person is likely to say to the learner rather than mainly on what the learner should initiate.

- Predictable questions and replies
- Common service interactions and prompts
- Short conversational reactions
- Ordinary reductions, linking, and connected speech
- Expressions whose natural spoken form can be difficult to recognize from the written form

For each key multiword expression, give all five required fields, including Common Pronunciation or the explicit no-meaningful-difference statement. Explain the expected response when the interaction actually calls for one. Use realistic contemporary PT-PT. Do not invent extreme reductions merely to make speech seem difficult.

### Help Mode

Follow [HELP Behavior](help-reference.md#help-behavior) and [Version Check on HELP](help-reference.md#version-check-on-help). Those sections define the valid arguments, full-guide content, command-specific help, and retrieval rules. Do not maintain a separate HELP policy here.

## Translation Quality Check

Before returning Literal structure or Natural English, apply this checklist regardless of the original translation direction, including after English-to-Portuguese translation:

- Interpret the Portuguese target in context rather than by isolated dictionary substitution.
- Confirm one-for-one gloss alignment with every Portuguese orthographic word in its original order, with visible ` | ` between glosses (source Markdown `\|` per SKILL.md). Correct any reordering, merging, omission, addition, or missing separator.
- Apply the [native-collocation check](#native-collocation-and-idiomatic-english-check) to Natural English. Use normal contemporary American English, neither an unnatural dictionary-by-dictionary rendering nor an unnecessarily less literal alternative.
- Preserve the source's register, tone, and intended meaning.
- For multiword targets, verify that the fifth field is present and represents the *same* Portuguese wording, with a nonrepeated unchanged statement where applicable.
